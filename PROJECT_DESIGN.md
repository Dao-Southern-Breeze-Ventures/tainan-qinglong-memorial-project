## Project Overview

Qinglong Temple is located at No. 59, Yuyong Road, East District, **Tainan City, Taiwan**. Its main deity is Marshal Hsieh (Hsieh Yung-chang, also documented as Hsieh Xuan). Secondary deities include Earth God, Goddess of Childbirth, General Yoshihara and General Zhao Sheng. Its religious origin traces back to Hsieh Yung-chang, a vanguard officer who perished in combat during Koxinga's 1661 military expedition expelling Dutch forces from Taiwan. Over the following three centuries, the land around Tainan experienced successive upheavals: the fall of the Kingdom of Tungning, the Yi-wei Resistance, armed anti-Japanese movements, and Taiwanese conscripts drawn into imperial wars.

The expansion of Qinglong Temple creates a physical space for commemoration, narration and inheritance of this historical thread spanning maritime expeditions, colonial resistance and wartime trauma. The temple currently occupies approximately 60 ping. Site preparation, main-hall expansion and new Memorial-Tower construction are planned. Phase-one capacity will be 16,000 tablet niches. Combined with film-video productions and the Parallel-World App, this forms an integrated project covering religious culture, memorial services and digital remembrance.

Startup capital will be raised via **Participatory Investment Crowdfunding**. Each subscription unit costs 800 USD. The project activates upon reaching 1,200 units, with an upper cap of 3,600 units. Co-design participants obtain returns under selected schemes and share motion-picture copyright revenue as well as allocated net operating profit from the App.

This document is not a sales pitch. It breaks the project into discussable, contributable, version-controlled design modules for community contribution on GitHub.

Project sequence: **Design-solicitation Phase → Launch Participatory Investment Crowdfunding → Execution**. Post-launch work including architecture, tablet-niche delivery, film-video and App development proceeds in parallel within the execution phase.

**Establishment Background Summary**

In the 15th year of Yongli (1661), Hsieh Yung-chang (style name Pingshan), a native of Quanzhou, Fujian, served as vanguard cavalry troop commander under Koxinga's expeditionary army. He was struck by a poisoned arrow in battle and ascended at the hour of Wei on 24 August of the same year, aged 32. He later manifested miracles protecting local people. In 1962, his remains were unearthed on the present-day Zhongshan Junior High School campus. Under former Mayor Lin Shan-shan and the Civil Engineering Section Chief, shrine foundations were laid and Qinglong Temple founded. Behind the temple rest 1,067 bone relics, including those of Japanese General Yoshihara and Qing-dynasty General Zhao Sheng, venerated as left- and right-hand assistant deities. Temple construction proceeded in the 1950s; a grand thanksgiving purification ritual was held in 1972.

## Historical Context & Religious Heritage

Marshal Hsieh worshipped at Qinglong Temple is not a deity detached from history. His story originates from cross-ocean warfare. Tainan in Taiwan repeatedly became a frontier battlefield of successive empires. This section incorporates four connected historical episodes as the cultural foundation for Memorial-Tower narratives, film-video content and the App.

### 1. 1661-1662: Koxinga versus Dutch Forces in Taiwan

In April 1661, Koxinga led roughly 25,000 troops and hundreds of war vessels departing from Liaoluo Bay, Kinmen, via the Penghu Islands toward Taiwan, landing at Luermen. He subsequently besieged Fort Zeelandia (the modern Anping Fort area). Dutch Governor Coyett surrendered on 1 February 1662 after exhausting supplies, ending 38 years of Dutch colonial rule over Taiwan. Marshal Hsieh (Hsieh Yung-chang) served as vanguard cavalry troop commander under Koxinga's forces. He died from poison-arrow wounds on 24 August 1661, aged 32. **The Marshal Hsieh faith directly originates from this campaign.**

### 2. 1683: Shi Lang's Invasion of Taiwan

After establishing rule over Taiwan, the Kingdom of Tungning stood in cross-strait confrontation against the Qing court for more than twenty-two years. In 1683 (Kangxi Year 22), Fujian Naval Admiral Shi Lang led over 200 warships and 20,000 soldiers, setting sail from Dongshan amid prevailing south-westerly winds. His fleet defeated the Tungning navy commanded by Liu Kuo-hsuan after seven days of fierce combat in Penghu waters. With Penghu fallen, Taiwan proper lay exposed. Zheng Ke-shuang surrendered later that year, bringing the Tungning regime to an end.

For Qinglong Temple, the significance lies not purely in regime change. **Tainan entered a new imperial order, while Marshal Hsieh's faith persisted within local society as a stabilising force across dynastic transitions.**

### 3. 1895-1930: Taiwanese Resistance against Japanese Rule

After the Qing ceded Taiwan to Japan under the Treaty of Shimonoseki in 1895, Taiwanese people mounted armed resistance. This includes the **Yi-wei War of 1895** defending the short-lived Republic of Formosa, subsequent years of guerrilla uprisings, and the 1915 **Tapani Incident**. Han-ethnic armed resistance persisted for two decades. In 1930, Seediq chief Mona Rudao led the **Wushe Incident**, raiding Japanese police outposts. The uprising lasted more than two months before being suppressed with poison-gas weapons; hundreds of Seediq fighters died or committed suicide.

**These acts of resistance unfolded on the same land where Qinglong Temple stands, resonating across more than two centuries with Marshal Hsieh's ethos of defending the homeland and protecting the people.**

### 4. Early 20th Century: Taiwanese Conscripts in Far-Eastern Wars and the Siberian Prisoner-of-War History

During the 1918-1922 Siberian Intervention, Taiwan under Japanese colonial rule dispatched no independent Taiwanese army unit. Large-scale Taiwanese involvement in Russian battlefields occurred during and after WWII. Roughly 200,000 Taiwanese were mobilised as soldiers and civilian auxiliaries under Japanese rule. Some were disarmed by Soviet forces post-war and dispatched to forced labour camps in Siberia; at least 19 known Taiwanese POWs survived that ordeal.

This project memorialises not triumphal imperial war narratives, **but the trauma of Taiwanese people conscripted to fight for foreign empires and abandoned after hostilities ended**. This forms a historical parallel with Hsieh Yung-chang's overseas sacrifice in 1661: for three centuries, Taiwanese bodies were repeatedly drawn into wars not of their own making. The expansion of Qinglong Temple creates a commemorative space for these dispersed, silenced souls.

## Participatory Investment Crowdfunding Scheme

### Base Rules


| Item                        | Content                                                             |
|-----------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Price per subscription unit | 800 USD                                                             |
| Activation threshold        | 1,200 units (960,000 USD startup capital)                           |
| Maximum cap                 | 3,600 units (2,880,000 USD)                                         |
| Participation ratio         | Participant's subscription units ÷ total issued subscription units |
| Base return                 | Scheme 1 or Scheme 2 (mutually exclusive)                           |
| Bonus revenue pool          | 30% of motion-picture copyright revenue pool                        |
| Ten-year revenue pool       | 10% of Parallel-World App net operating profit pool                 |


### Base-Return Schemes (Choose One)

**Scheme 1: Tablet-Sales Revenue Sharing (Distribution-Pool Model)**

- For each tablet niche sold, 10% of that niche's sales price is contributed into the distribution pool in the same month.
- Payments are processed monthly: revenue realised in-month is distributed in-month, allocated through the distribution pool according to each participant's participation ratio.
- No payment is generated for unsold niches.
- Calculation formula:

```
Participant-i monthly Scheme-1 payout  
= (Total monthly tablet-niche sales revenue × 10%) × (Participant-i owned units ÷ Total outstanding subscription units)
```

> 
> Example: 20 triple-stack apartment-style niches sold in a month (unit price TWD 70,000) = total sales revenue TWD 1,400,000;
> 10% contributed to the pool = TWD 140,000;
> if a participant holds 10 units out of 1,200 total units, that participant's monthly payout is `140,000 × (10 ÷ 1,200) ≈ TWD 1,166.7` (foreign-currency settlement per prevailing exchange rate and formal legal documents).
> 

**Scheme 2: One-Year Lump-Sum Payback**

- One calendar year after project activation, each unit receives a one-off lump-sum repayment of 1,200 USD.
- Payment workflows and tax treatment shall follow formal documents reviewed by Canadian legal counsel.

### Participant Bonus Scheme (Motion Picture)

- 30% of copyright revenue generated during the four-month window prior to film release flows into the participant distribution pool.
- All participants receive allocations proportional to their participation ratio.
- Disbursement occurs six months after official film release.
- The precise definition and accounting scope of "copyright revenue in the four months pre-release" is an open-design topic; the final definition shall be set within formal legal documentation.

### Participant Ten-Year Revenue (Parallel-World App)

- 10% of Parallel-World App net operating profit flows annually into the participant distribution pool.
- All participants receive allocations proportional to their participation ratio.
- Disbursement follows completion of annual audits, for a period of ten years.
- Definition of net operating profit, appointed audit institution and accounting rules are subject to formal legal documents.

> 
> General pool-allocation formula:
> 
> 
> 
> 
```
> Participant-i pool allocation = Total current-period pool amount × (Participant-i owned units ÷ Total outstanding subscription units)
```

### File Naming & Writing Specifications

1. **Filenames**: always English lowercase, hyphen-separated; no Chinese characters, spaces or special symbols (e.g. `memorial-tower-concept-01.png`).
2. **Encoding**: all Markdown files use UTF-8.
3. **Bilingual documents**: Chinese uses the `.zh.md` suffix, English uses `.md` (consistent with existing root files).
4. **Images**: stored under the matching subdirectory of `assets/`, referenced by relative paths, e.g. `![Memorial Tower concept](assets/architectural-sketches/memorial-tower-concept-01.png)`.
5. **Numerical changes**: any change to prices, unit counts, ratios or allocation formulas must update `crowdfunding/distribution-rules.md` and the calculator scripts in the same Pull Request, listing the affected scope.
6. **Version marking**: major design changes retain a version number in the document title (this document is v0.3); history is traceable through Git commits.
